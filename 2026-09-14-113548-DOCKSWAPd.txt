⏺ Here is a simple re-pitch.

  The goal. You want a new DockSwap feature. You want to select apps and put
  them into a visual group. The group works like a real Dock folder.

  The plan. We use a Wayfinder map to plan this feature. A Wayfinder map is a
  GitHub issue. It tracks the open questions as tickets. We made three tickets:

  1. Ticket #3 — Decide how to store group data in the preset file. (Done. 
     Closed.)
  2. Ticket #4 — Test if the group folder will work in the real Dock. (In 
     progress now.)
  3. Ticket #5 — Design the drag-and-drop feel in the editor app. (Not started.)

  Why ticket #4 exists. A real Dock folder points at a directory (a folder) on
  disk. DockSwap wants to build that directory from apps that live in different
  places (like /Applications). DockSwap cannot move or copy the apps. So
  DockSwap must put "pointer" files in the new directory instead. Each pointer
  file points at one real app.

  There are two kinds of pointer file on a Mac:

❯ explain in detail each option /wayfinder                                      

⏺ Here's each option in more detail — how it'd actually feel to use in
  DockSwapEditor:

  1. Direct merge
  You grab an app row (say, Safari) and drag it directly on top of another app
  row (Calculator). The moment you release over it, both rows collapse into a
  single new group row right there in the list — no separate area, no waiting. A
  small inline text field appears immediately asking for the group's name; type
  it and hit enter (or it defaults to something like "Safari, Calculator" until
  renamed).
  - Feel: fast, direct, minimal ceremony — two actions (drag, name) and you're
    done.
  - Cost: least forgiving — an accidental drag onto the wrong row instantly
    creates an unwanted group. Also the trickiest to build correctly (SwiftUI
    drop-target detection has to be precise about which row you're over).

  2. Staging tray
  A persistent drop zone (a card or panel, maybe labeled "New Group," sitting
  above or beside the list) stays visible while you're in a drag. You drag apps
  into it one at a time — Safari first, then Calculator, then a third if you
  want — and watch them collect visually in the tray. Nothing becomes a group
  until you explicitly click "Create" (or similar), at which point you're
  prompted for a name and the tray empties back to its idle state.
  - Feel: deliberate, safe — you can change your mind mid-build (remove an app
    from the tray before committing), and it naturally supports grouping 3+ apps
    without extra drags-onto-a-group later.
  - Cost: more UI surface (a whole new panel), more steps for the simple 2-app
    case, and it's a less "native macOS" feeling interaction — more reminiscent
    of a shopping cart than a Dock.

  3. Hover-to-combine
  Same starting gesture as Direct merge — drag one app onto another — but
  dropping isn't instant. As you hover over the target row, a visual cue builds
  (e.g. the target row highlights, maybe a small ring or pulse animates in) over
  a short beat (150–250ms), and only completes the merge once that hold
  finishes. Release early and nothing happens.
  - Feel: mimics iOS's springboard "hold to fold apps into a folder" — feels
    familiar to anyone who's used an iPhone home screen, and the hold acts as a
    built-in confirmation that guards against accidental merges.
  - Cost: adds a timing element that has to feel right (too short and it's the
    same as Direct merge with extra jank; too long and it feels laggy) — the one
    variant most dependent on tuning, not just layout.

  For what it's worth, given this is your own Dock-preset tool (not a consumer
  app with novice users), I'd lean toward Direct merge — it's the least code,
  matches "intuitively" from your original ask, and the accidental-merge risk is
  low-stakes here (you can always drag an app back out or dissolve the group).
  But this is exactly the kind of thing worth actually seeing before deciding —
  that's what the /prototype run is for.
